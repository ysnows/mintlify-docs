The Enconvo iPhone app pairs with your Mac, so your Mac's projects and chats are on your phone. Every chat you open from the phone keeps running on your Mac, with its files, apps, and agents. This tutorial pairs a phone from start to finish: sign in, show the pairing code on the Mac, and scan it with the iPhone.

<YouTubePlayer videoId="rYhgz_SjnNs" title="How to Pair Your iPhone with Your Mac in Enconvo | Step-by-Step Tutorial" />

The video is 1 minute 12 seconds long. The written steps below follow the same order, with the matching timestamp for each step.

<Note>
The iPhone app is in beta. Install it from [TestFlight](https://testflight.apple.com/join/MJQuEN28), or scan the TestFlight code under **Get the phone app** in **Settings → Connections** on your Mac. Pairing needs Enconvo 2.5.6 beta or later on the Mac. The iPhone app on TestFlight and the Android app on Google Play are available in the Americas, Europe, the Middle East, Japan, Korea, and Southeast Asia. The Android APK installs anywhere.
</Note>

## Pair your iPhone

To pair with a Windows PC, a Linux desktop or a Linux server with only a terminal, see [Pair with your phone](/integrations/pair-your-phone).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Sign in on your iPhone (0:07)">
    Install the iPhone app and sign in with the same Enconvo account as your Mac: with Apple, with Google, or with a 6-digit code emailed to you. Only phones signed in to your account can pair with it. A free account can pair, use the Mac's chats and Remote Screen, and use the Avatar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Show the pairing code (0:16)">
    On the Mac, open **Settings → Connections** and select **Show pairing code**. The QR code is good for five minutes. If it runs out, select **Show a new code**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scan it with your iPhone (0:22)">
    On the phone, open **Remote** and tap **Pair a computer** (**Pair a Mac** in the video), then point the camera at the code on your Mac. The iPhone Camera works too: it opens Enconvo, and you confirm the Mac there. The two pair in a moment. Your phone reaches the Mac through the Enconvo relay, and messages are end-to-end encrypted.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your Mac, on your phone (0:34)">
    Once the Mac shows as **Online** in Remote, its projects and chats are right there on your phone.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open any chat (0:40)">
    Open any chat and pick up where you left off. The chat still runs on your Mac, so it can use that Mac's files, apps, and agents.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep it reachable (0:47)">
    In **Connections** (or **Settings → Keep Awake**), turn on **Keep this Mac awake**. It keeps the Mac from sleeping, on battery too, so your phone can always reach it. You can also keep the screen on, have it turn off after a set time, or keep the Mac awake with the lid closed (this asks for an administrator password once).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Remove a phone (0:52)">
    Lost a phone? In **Connections → Paired phones**, select **Remove** next to it. That phone can no longer reach your Mac. To use it again, pair it with a new code.
  </Step>
</Steps>

When the Mac says so under the code, scanning it also signs the iPhone in to your Enconvo account. Only scan such a code with your own iPhone.

## What you can do from your phone

Once paired, you can:

- Browse your Mac's projects and chats, and continue any chat.
- Answer questions an Agent asks while it works on your Mac.
- Get notified when a chat finishes, and reply from the notification.
- See and control your Mac's screen with **Remote Screen**.
- Chat with AI models on the phone itself, using Enconvo Cloud points, your own API key, or a plan you sign in to (ChatGPT, Grok, Le Chat, GitHub Copilot, or Antigravity). See [Phone Chats](/integrations/iphone-phone-chats).
- Chat and call with friends and teammates, and join a Video Conference. See [Calls and Friends on Your Phone](/integrations/phone-calls-and-friends).

The app calls a paired Mac, Windows PC or Linux machine your **computer**. The Android app works the same way unless a section says otherwise.

## Work in your computer's chats

A chat from your computer looks and works as it does there, with background tasks, Subagents and steering.

- **Home** shows your active chats right above the message box. Swipe the card's grabber up, or tap **All**, to see every active chat grouped by what needs you, what is running and what has results.
- The sidebar's **Active** group gathers the chats waiting on you, failed, still running or holding an unread result. A blue dot marks a result nobody has read, and a turning ring a running chat. **Pinned** and **Recent** fold.
- What you send while a reply runs steers the running task, as Return does on the Mac. The box reads “Steer the running task…” meanwhile.
- Tap a Subagent to open its steps in a sheet over the chat. It opens at the newest step and follows new output. Swipe down to close it.
- Approval questions offer the agent's own choices, as on the Mac.
- To change a chat's agent, tap the agent under the chat's title. **Fork from here** lets you pick the agent for the fork.
- The model sheet shows your subscription's plan, its 5-hour and weekly limits with what's left and when they reset, and a model's own limit on its row.
- Attach photos and videos from the photo library. Videos go to the computer as they are.
- In the app's **Settings → Avatar**, choose whether the message box's call button shows the avatar or the voice bars icon.

When your computer is offline or still reconnecting, you can still send: the phone answers what it can, such as questions and web lookups, and sends messages that need the computer once it connects.

A document or link the phone can't open says to open it on your computer.

## Use your computer's screen

Tap the display icon at the top right of **Home**, or **Remote Screen** in a chat's menu, to see your computer's screen and control it.

| Mode | What a touch does |
| --- | --- |
| **Trackpad** | Slide anywhere to move the pointer, and tap to click where the pointer is. Hints teach the gestures as you use them. |
| **Touch** | Tap where you want to click. |
| **Just Watch** | Nothing: you only watch. |

The mode button shows the current mode's name. Pinch to zoom; in **Trackpad** the picture zooms around the pointer and follows it.

To keep the screen floating while you do something else, tap the Picture in Picture button at the top left, or pinch in. The screen floats over your chats, and over other apps when you leave Enconvo.

During a Live Talk call, the **Remote Screen** tool opens the screen in a small floating window over the call. It works like a trackpad, with a mode chip to switch to **Touch** or **Just Watch**, a keyboard button to type on the computer, and **Full Screen** to open the usual view.

## Notifications

- A finished reply's notification shows the start of the reply. Use **Reply** on it to answer without opening the app.
- No notification comes while the app is open, or while you're using your Mac, unless you sent that message from the phone.
- Once you read a finished chat on one device, its notification disappears from your others. A question still waiting for an answer stays.
- On Android 14 and later, turn on **Full-screen notifications** in the app's Settings if Android turned them off.

## Share into Enconvo

Share a page, a photo or a file into Enconvo from another app, and the **Send to** sheet lets you pick a new chat or any recent chat. Turn on **Always share to new chat** in the sheet, or in the app's Settings, to skip the question.

## Projects and artifacts

- Open a Project's **Ideas** and **Board**, change its settings, start a **New Project**, and act on a Work plan card: answer, merge, start or retry a task, open a pull request, or write down and start an idea.
- The `@` menu lists the Project's members, Workflows and the Agents that can join.
- Decks, dashboards and Motion films open from a chat or the Artifacts library, where you can comment, ask @Enconvo, share, export and present.

## Android app

The Android app pairs and works the same way. Sign in with Google or a 6-digit code emailed to you. The message box sits on one row, with a camera button, quick chips for the computer, the Project and **Skills**, and a plus panel with recent photos and videos.

The Android app downloaded from enconvo.com checks for updates each time you open it. **Skip This Version** hides a version until a newer one comes out; **Later** hides the alert until you next open the app.

## Manage paired phones

**Settings → Connections** lists every paired phone and shows whether phones can reach this Mac right now.

| Task | How |
| --- | --- |
| Pair another phone | Select **Pair another phone** and scan the new code. A Mac can have up to 10 paired phones. |
| Rename a phone | Click the phone, then click **Rename phone** next to its name. |
| Remove a phone | Select **Remove** next to it in **Paired phones**. |
| Start over | Select **Reset connection**. Enconvo creates a new connection key and removes every paired phone. Each phone then scans a new code. |

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| The phone can't reach your Mac | Check that the Mac is awake, signed in, and online, and that **Connections** shows it as **Online**. Turn on **Keep this Mac awake** so it stays reachable. |
| The phone says the Mac refused a message, the screen or the terminal | The Mac has no Device access for it. Follow the steps the phone shows. |
| The code says **This code expired** | Select **Show a new code** and scan again within five minutes. |
| The Mac says **Sign in to pair a phone** | Click **Sign in** and sign in on the Mac. Your iPhone and your Mac need to use the same Enconvo account. |
| The Mac says it **needs a new connection key** | The relay has a different key for this Mac, for example after you restored it from a backup. Select **Reset connection**, then pair each phone again. |
| **Companion access was turned off** | Access was turned off from one of your devices, and no phone can reach your Macs until you select **Turn access back on**. |
